## What Is Small Business Bookkeeping?

Bookkeeping is the process of recording and organizing a business's financial transactions. It creates an organized record of the money coming into and going out of your business.

Typical bookkeeping tasks include:

- Recording sales and business income.
- Tracking expenses and purchases.
- Organizing invoices and receipts.
- Monitoring unpaid customer invoices.
- Recording bills and payments.
- Reconciling bank and credit card accounts.
- Maintaining records for applicable tax reporting.

Bookkeeping and accounting are related, but they are not identical. Bookkeeping focuses on recording and organizing transactions. Accounting can involve interpreting financial information, preparing financial statements, and providing broader financial or tax advice.

Good bookkeeping gives business owners a more reliable foundation for understanding their financial position.

## What Records Should a Small Business Keep?

The records required depend on your business structure and activities. However, many businesses benefit from organizing the following documents.

| Record type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Income records | Sales invoices, receipts, payment confirmations |
| Expense records | Supplier invoices, receipts, purchase records |
| Banking records | Bank statements, credit card statements |
| Customer records | Outstanding invoices and payment histories |
| Payroll records | Wage information and related documents, where applicable |
| Tax records | Relevant GST/HST filings and supporting documents |
| Asset records | Equipment purchases and disposal information |
| Business documents | Contracts and other transaction-related agreements |

The CRA generally requires businesses to keep records for at least six years from the end of the last tax year to which they relate, although exceptions and specific circumstances may apply. Consult the [CRA's recordkeeping guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records.html) for details.

## Common Bookkeeping Mistakes to Avoid

Even a busy business owner can improve their recordkeeping by avoiding a few common mistakes.

- **Mixing personal and business transactions:** Separate records make it easier to identify business income and expenses.
- **Falling behind on transactions:** Record transactions regularly rather than allowing months of paperwork to accumulate.
- **Losing receipts:** Store supporting documents in a consistent, accessible filing system.
- **Ignoring unpaid invoices:** Review outstanding customer payments so you know which amounts still need follow-up.
- **Skipping bank reconciliations:** Compare recorded transactions with bank statements to identify missing entries or discrepancies.
- **Waiting until tax season:** Organize records throughout the year to make reporting preparation easier.

## A Simple Monthly Bookkeeping Checklist

Use this checklist to build a consistent routine.

1. Record all income and expenses.
2. Organize receipts and supplier invoices.
3. Review unpaid customer invoices.
4. Record bills and payments.
5. Reconcile bank and credit card accounts.
6. Review missing or unclear transactions.
7. Organize relevant tax records.
8. Back up digital financial documents.

The exact process will depend on the size and complexity of your business. Consistency matters more than using an unnecessarily complicated system.
