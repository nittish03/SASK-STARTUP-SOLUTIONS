You have legally formed a corporate entity under Saskatchewan law, complete with a unique Saskatchewan Corporate Entity Number and the statutory protection of limited liability. In reality, receiving the Certificate of Incorporation is merely the legal birth of your company. ## What Happens Immediately After You Incorporate in Saskatchewan?

Immediately after you incorporate in Saskatchewan, your business becomes an independent legal entity under *The Business Corporations Act, 2021*, but it lacks legal organization until the initial directors pass organizational resolutions, adopt bylaws, issue shares to founders, and establish the corporate minute book. Until these foundational corporate documents are executed, the corporation cannot legally issue shares, validate director signing authorities, or satisfy Canadian anti-money laundering banking requirements.

When Information Services Corporation (ISC) approves your Articles of Incorporation (Form 1), it issues:
1. **Certificate of Incorporation:** The formal statutory certificate confirming the legal name, Saskatchewan Entity Number, and effective incorporation date.
2. **Approved Articles of Incorporation:** The foundational legal charter setting out share classes, share transfer restrictions, and registered office requirements.

However, ISC does not create your internal corporate bylaws, does not issue your share certificates, does not notify the Canada Revenue Agency to open your tax sub-accounts, and does not register your business with the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance for Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Those responsibilities fall entirely upon the directors and officers of the newly formed company.

## Why Is a Corporate Minute Book Legally Required in Saskatchewan?

A Corporate Minute Book is legally required in Saskatchewan because Section 20 of *The Business Corporations Act, 2021* mandates that every corporation maintain complete corporate records at its registered office, including its Articles, bylaws, director resolutions, shareholder registers, and share certificates. Operating a corporation without a properly maintained minute book violates provincial corporate statute, undermines the corporation’s limited liability protection, and prevents financial institutions or investors from validating corporate ownership.

Many founders believe that a corporate minute book is an outdated relic of the paper era. In modern Saskatchewan corporate governance, whether stored in a physical leather binder or maintained as an authenticated digital corporate record, the minute book is the single legal source of truth for your business.

Without an up-to-date Corporate Minute Book:
- **Commercial Banks Will Freeze or Refuse Accounts:** Canadian financial institutions cannot verify who holds signing authority, who owns voting shares, or who satisfies federal Beneficial Ownership transparency rules.
- **Financing and Grants Are Blocked:** Commercial lenders (such as BDC, Futurpreneur, or chartered banks) and provincial granting bodies (such as Innovation Saskatchewan) require corporate searches and certified minute book excerpts before disbursing funds.
- **Due Diligence Fails During Sale or Investment:** Prospective investors or purchasers cannot verify whether shares were validly issued, creating severe legal roadblocks during equity financing or business acquisitions.
- **Limited Liability Is Vulnerable:** In commercial litigation, plaintiffs can argue that the corporation failed to observe statutory corporate formalities, increasing the risk of personal liability for the owners.

## What Documents Must Be Included in a Saskatchewan Corporate Minute Book?

A Saskatchewan Corporate Minute Book must include the Certificate and Articles of Incorporation, organizational resolutions of directors and shareholders, General Operating Bylaw No. 1, signed Director Consents, issued Share Certificates, the Central Securities Register, the Register of Directors and Officers, and the newly mandated Register of Individuals with Significant Control (ISC Register). Together, these statutory documents verify the corporate governance, capitalization, and legal authority of the company.

```

├────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┤

│  • ISC Corporate Profile Report│  • Shareholder Resolutions     │
├────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│      Securities & Ownership    │     Statutory Registers        │
│  • Share Subscription Forms    │  • Central Securities Register │
│  • Numbered Share Certificates │  • Register of Directors & Officers│
│  • Capitalization Ledger       │  • Significant Control Register│
└────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

The essential components required within every Saskatchewan corporate minute book include:

### 1. Certificate and Articles of Incorporation
A certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued by ISC, accompanied by the official Articles of Incorporation (Form 1) detailing authorized share classes (e.g., Class A Voting Common, Class B Non-Voting Common, Class C Preferred Shares) and any statutory restrictions on business activities or share transfers.

### 2. General Operating Bylaw No. 1
Bylaws are the operational rules governing the corporation's internal affairs. Bylaw No. 1 establishes:
- Procedures for calling and conducting meetings of directors and shareholders.
- Quorum requirements and voting majorities.
- Corporate banking authorities and execution of commercial instruments.
- Appointment, powers, and duties of corporate officers (President, Secretary, Treasurer).
- Indemnification of directors and officers against commercial lawsuits.

### 3. Director Consents and First Organizational Resolutions
Under Saskatchewan law, individuals named as initial directors in the Articles must sign a formal **Consent to Act as Director**. The initial directors then pass the **Organizational Resolutions of Directors**, which:
- Formally adopt General Operating Bylaw No. 1.
- Approve the corporate seal (if adopted) and corporate share certificate designs.
- Appoint corporate officers (e.g., electing Patrick Vachon as President and Secretary).
- Authorize the issuance of shares to the initial founding shareholders in exchange for consideration (cash or property transferred).
- Designate the corporation’s primary financial institution and authorize corporate banking signatories.
- Fix the corporate fiscal year-end (e.g., March 31, June 30, or December 31).

### 4. Shareholder Resolutions and Share Subscription Agreements
Founding shareholders execute **Share Subscription Agreements**, confirming the number and class of shares purchased, the subscription price paid per share (e.g., 100 Class A Common Shares at \$1.00 per share), and payment in full. The shareholders then pass resolutions ratifying the bylaws and confirming the election of the Board of Directors.

### 5. Numbered Corporate Share Certificates
Share certificates are physical or cryptographic legal evidence of share ownership. Each certificate must state:
- The legal name of the Saskatchewan corporation.
- That the corporation is governed by the laws of Saskatchewan.
- The name of the registered shareholder.
- The number and specific class of shares represented.
- Formal signatures of authorized corporate officers (President and Secretary).

### 6. Central Securities Register (Shareholders Ledger)
A statutory ledger recording the complete capitalization history of the corporation, including the name and address of each shareholder, the number and class of shares held, the certificate number issued, the date of issuance, and the consideration received by the corporation.

### 7. Register of Directors and Officers
A detailed historical log listing every individual who has served as a director or officer, including their full legal name, residential address, Canadian citizenship/residency status, date of election or appointment, and date on which they ceased to hold office.

### 8. Register of Individuals with Significant Control (ISC Transparency Register)
Under modernized Canadian and Saskatchewan corporate transparency legislation (*The Business Corporations Act, 2021*), every private Saskatchewan corporation must maintain a dedicated **Register of Individuals with Significant Control (ISC)**. An individual with significant control is anyone who:
- Owns, controls, or directs 25% or more of the voting rights attached to the corporation's outstanding shares; or
- Owns, controls, or directs 25% or more of all outstanding shares measured by fair market value; or
- Has direct or indirect influence that, if exercised, would result in factual control of the corporation.

The register must record the individual's full legal name, date of birth, country of citizenship, residential address, tax identification number (SIN), the date they acquired significant control, and a description of how that control is exercised. Failing to maintain this register can result in statutory fines of up to \$5,000 for the corporation and up to \$50,000 (or imprisonment) for directors.

## How Do You Open a Canadian Business Bank Account for a Saskatchewan Corporation?

To open a Canadian business bank account for a Saskatchewan corporation, you must present the official Certificate of Incorporation, certified Articles of Incorporation, an up-to-date ISC Corporate Registry Search or Profile Report, and the corporate Banking Resolution from your minute book to an authorized financial institution. Canadian banks are legally required under federal anti-money laundering (AML) legislation and FINTRAC regulations to verify the corporation's legal existence, active standing, and beneficial owners holding 25% or more equity before opening commercial accounts.

You can establish corporate accounts with major Canadian chartered banks (such as RBC Royal Bank, TD Canada Trust, Scotiabank, BMO Bank of Montreal, or CIBC) or leading Saskatchewan credit unions (such as Affinity Credit Union or Conexus Credit Union).

### Mandatory Documentation Checklist for Banking:
When attending your corporate commercial banking appointment in Saskatoon, bring the following complete documentation package:
1. **ISC Certificate of Incorporation:** The original electronic certificate bearing the provincial seal and registration number.
2. **Approved Articles of Incorporation (Form 1):** Showing share classes and authorized corporate restrictions.
3. **ISC Corporate Profile Report / Corporate Search:** Dated within the last 30 to 60 days, proving that the corporation is in active legal standing and has not been struck or dissolved.
4. **Corporate Banking Resolution & Minute Book Excerpts:** A signed director resolution authorizing the specific financial institution, confirming the account types, and identifying authorized signatories.
5. **Beneficial Ownership Transparency Declaration:** A completed form disclosing all individuals who own or control 25% or more of the corporate shares.
6. **Government Identification:** Two valid pieces of government-issued ID (such as a Canadian Passport, Saskatchewan Driver's Licence, or Permanent Resident Card) for every director, officer, and authorized account signatory.

> **Critical Rule:** Never deposit corporate revenue into a personal bank account or write personal cheques for corporate obligations. Commingling funds destroys the corporate veil, exposes your personal estate to business lawsuits, and triggers severe CRA shareholder loan tax penalties.

## How Do You Register CRA Business Number and Program Accounts in Saskatchewan?

You register your CRA Business Number and program accounts by contacting the Canada Revenue Agency through Business Registration Online (BRO) or via phone immediately following incorporation to obtain a 9-digit federal Business Number (BN9) and the required program identifier suffixes. A Saskatchewan corporation requires a Corporate Income Tax account (RC0001), and must add a GST/HST account (RT0001) and Payroll account (RP0001) as operational activities dictate.

The federal Business Number is a unique, nine-digit identification number assigned to your corporate entity. Attached to this nine-digit root are two-letter program identifiers and four-digit reference numbers:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         CRA Federal Business Number Structure          │
├───────────────────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────┤
│  9-Digit Entity   │  Program Code    │ Account Number  │
│    123456789      │       RC         │      0001       │
│  (Specific to     │  (Corporate Tax) │  (First Account)│
│   Corporation)    │                  │                 │
└───────────────────┴──────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

The four essential CRA corporate program accounts include:

### 1. Corporate Income Tax Account (RC0001)
The Corporate Income Tax program account is automatically assigned or activated when your corporation is registered with the CRA. This account is mandatory for submitting your annual [T2 Corporate Income Tax Return](/services#tax). Every incorporated Saskatchewan company must file an annual T2 return within six months of its fiscal year-end, even if the corporation earned zero revenue or was temporarily inactive.

### 2. GST/HST Program Account (RT0001)
Under federal tax law, your corporation must register for a GST/HST account if its gross taxable worldwide sales exceed **\$30,000** within a single calendar quarter or across four consecutive calendar quarters.
- **Voluntary Early Registration:** Even if your revenue is currently under \$30,000, registering immediately upon incorporation is highly advantageous. Early registration allows your corporation to claim **Input Tax Credits (ITCs)**, recovering the full 5% GST paid on startup capital expenses, equipment purchases, website development, professional fees, and initial inventory.

### 3. Payroll Deductions Account (RP0001)
If your corporation plans to pay salaries, hourly wages, or executive bonuses to employees or owner-managers, you must open a Payroll Deductions account before the first payroll run. As an employer, the corporation is legally required to:
- Withhold Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions, Employment Insurance (EI) premiums, and federal/provincial income taxes from employee gross wages.
- Remit the withheld deductions—along with the mandatory employer matching portions of CPP (100% match) and EI (140% match)—to the CRA by the 15th day of the month following the payroll cycle.
- File annual **T4 Information Slips** and a **T4 Summary** with the CRA on or before the last day of February each year.

### 4. Information Returns Account (RZ0001)
If the corporation intends to distribute corporate profits to shareholders as dividends rather than paying a regular salary, you will need an Information Returns account to issue annual **T5 Information Slips** (Statement of Investment Income) and a **T5 Summary** to shareholders and the CRA by the end of February.

## What Provincial Accounts Must You Set Up in Saskatchewan?

In Saskatchewan, you must set up a Provincial Sales Tax (PST) vendor account through the Ministry of Finance's Saskatchewan Electronic Tax Service (SETS) and register with the Saskatchewan Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) if operating in a mandatory coverage industry or hiring workers. In addition, you must obtain a municipal business licence from the City of Saskatoon or your local municipality before opening your doors to the public.

Provincial compliance operates independently from federal CRA registrations:

### 1. Saskatchewan Provincial Sales Tax (PST) via SETS
In Saskatchewan, Provincial Sales Tax is a 6% retail sales tax applied to taxable goods and specific taxable services under *The Provincial Sales Tax Act*.
- **No Small Supplier Exemption:** Unlike the federal GST—which provides a \$30,000 threshold—Saskatchewan has **no small supplier exemption for PST**. Any corporation making retail sales of taxable goods or services in Saskatchewan must register for a PST vendor licence prior to commencing sales.
- **Registration Process:** Register online via the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance's **SETS (Saskatchewan Electronic Tax Service)** portal. Once approved, the Ministry issues a Saskatchewan PST Vendor Licence Number.
- **Filing Frequency:** Depending on estimated annual collections, the Ministry assigns a monthly, quarterly, or annual filing frequency. All collected PST must be remitted electronically through SETS.

### 2. Saskatchewan Workers' Compensation Board (WCB)
The Saskatchewan Workers' Compensation Board administers a compulsory, no-fault workplace injury insurance system under *The Workers' Compensation Act, 2013*.
- **Mandatory Registration:** If your corporation employs full-time, part-time, temporary, or casual workers—or if you operate in mandatory industries such as construction, transportation, forestry, manufacturing, or trades—you must register with WCB within **30 days** of commencing operations or hiring your first worker.
- **Director / Executive Coverage:** In Saskatchewan, corporate directors and executive officers who work in the business are generally considered workers under the Act unless an exemption applies. Directors can also apply for Optional Personal Coverage to ensure workplace injury wage replacement.

### 3. Municipal Business Licences (City of Saskatoon)
Under City of Saskatoon bylaws, any person or corporation carrying on a business, trade, or profession within city limits must hold an active municipal business licence.
- **Commercial Location Licence:** Required if leasing or owning a retail, industrial, or commercial office space.
- **Home-Based Business Licence:** Required if operating a professional consultancy, trades business, or tech firm from a private Saskatoon residence.

## Why Must You Set Up a Double-Entry Bookkeeping System from Day One?

You must set up a double-entry bookkeeping system from day one because corporations are subject to rigorous balance sheet accounting standards, strict shareholder loan tracking under CRA rules, and complex corporate tax reporting that cannot be managed with spreadsheets. Setting up professional bookkeeping immediately upon incorporation prevents expensive year-end accounting cleanups, ensures accurate sales tax tracking, and establishes audit-proof records.

When you operated as an unincorporated sole proprietor, basic single-entry cash logs were often sufficient to fill out personal tax Form T2125. A corporation, however, operates under double-entry accrual accounting principles. Every transaction affects two ledgers: a debit to one account and a credit to another.

Key bookkeeping requirements unique to Saskatchewan corporations include:
- **Shareholder Equity and Loan Ledgers:** When you inject personal startup cash into the corporation, purchase equipment out-of-pocket, or withdraw corporate profits, these transactions must flow through a formal **Shareholder Loan Account** on the corporate balance sheet. Failing to reconcile this account monthly can trigger devastating CRA Section 15(2) tax penalties, where personal withdrawals are deemed taxable personal income.
- **Input Tax Credit (ITC) Reconciliations:** Accurately segregating 5% GST paid on operating expenses from 6% Saskatchewan PST paid (which is generally an expense rather than a credit) ensures you maximize GST refunds without under-remitting provincial sales taxes.
- **Fixed Asset & Depreciation Schedules:** Capital equipment, machinery, and office technology must be entered onto the balance sheet and depreciated according to CRA Capital Cost Allowance (CCA) class rules on the annual corporate tax return.
- **GIFI Mapping for Corporate Tax Returns:** At fiscal year-end, your corporate balance sheet and income statement must be mapped directly to CRA General Index of Financial Information (GIFI) codes for your [T2 corporate tax filing in Saskatchewan](/services#tax). Clean, structured books make year-end corporate tax preparation rapid and affordable.

## What Are the Ongoing Annual Compliance Obligations for Saskatchewan Corporations?

The ongoing annual compliance obligations for Saskatchewan corporations include filing an annual Corporate Return with Information Services Corporation (ISC) during your incorporation anniversary month, submitting an annual T2 Corporate Income Tax Return to the CRA within six months of fiscal year-end, paying corporate taxes within two or three months, and documenting an Annual General Meeting (AGM) in your minute book. Maintaining these four annual filings ensures your company retains its legal existence and remains in good standing.

| Annual Corporate Filing | Regulatory Authority | Statutory Filing Deadline | Payment Deadline | Non-Compliance Consequences |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ISC Corporate Annual Return** | Information Services Corporation | Last day of incorporation anniversary month | Due upon annual return filing | Administrative strike-off, loss of name, dissolved corporation |
| **T2 Corporate Income Tax Return** | Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) | 6 months following fiscal year-end | 2 to 3 months following fiscal year-end | 5% late-filing penalty + 1% per month; compounding daily interest |
| **GST/HST Remittance Return** | Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) | Monthly, quarterly, or 3 months post-year-end (annual) | Same as filing deadline | Punitive penalties, arbitrary assessments, director liability |
| **Saskatchewan PST Return** | SK Ministry of Finance (SETS) | Monthly, quarterly, or annually (per assigned cycle) | 20th day of the month following period | 10% penalty + interest; suspension of provincial vendor licence |
| **T4 / T5 Information Returns** | Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) | Last day of February each calendar year | Source deductions due 15th of each month | Late-filing penalties of \$100 to \$7,500 based on slip count |
| **Annual Minutes / AGM Resolutions** | Corporate Minute Book | Within 15 months of prior AGM | Not applicable (internal document) | Inability to prove corporate acts; legal vulnerability in disputes |

## Frequently Asked Questions About Post-Incorporation in Saskatchewan

### What is the deadline for filing an ISC Corporate Annual Return in Saskatchewan?
In Saskatchewan, your ISC Corporate Annual Return must be filed every year during the month immediately following your company's incorporation anniversary month. For example, if your corporation was incorporated on October 14, your annual return filing window opens on October 1 and must be submitted to Information Services Corporation (ISC) on or before November 30. Filing the return confirms your registered office address, director registry, and share structure. Failing to file triggers default warning notices, escalating late penalties, and eventual administrative dissolution of the corporation.

### Can I operate my business using my personal bank account while waiting to set up corporate banking?
No. Operating a corporation through a personal bank account is strongly discouraged by corporate lawyers, accountants, and tax authorities. Depositing corporate revenue into a personal account commingles personal and business assets, which can invalidate the limited liability protection granted by your incorporation. Furthermore, personal withdrawals of corporate funds that are not formally tracked as payroll wages (T4) or declared dividends (T5) are classified by the CRA as shareholder loans, which can trigger severe personal income tax liabilities under Section 15(2) of the *Income Tax Act*.

### What happens if I incorporate in Saskatchewan but fail to create a Corporate Minute Book?
If you incorporate in Saskatchewan but fail to organize a Corporate Minute Book, your corporation is legally deficient under *The Business Corporations Act, 2021*. While your Certificate of Incorporation proves the entity was created, you cannot legally prove who owns the company’s shares, who is authorized to bind the company to contracts, or who was elected as a director. Canadian banks may refuse to open or may freeze commercial accounts, commercial lenders will decline financing applications, and investors will reject equity purchases. Organizing your minute book immediately after incorporation establishes an audit-proof legal foundation.

### When do I need to register for Saskatchewan Provincial Sales Tax (PST)?
In Saskatchewan, you must register for a Provincial Sales Tax (PST) vendor licence before you make your first retail sale of taxable goods or services. Unlike the federal GST, which offers a \$30,000 small supplier threshold for new businesses, Saskatchewan PST (6%) has no small supplier exemption. Registration is completed online through the Saskatchewan Electronic Tax Service (SETS) portal administered by the Ministry of Finance.
