## How Does Saskatchewan PST Apply to Construction Contractors and Real Property Contracts?

In Saskatchewan, construction contractors are generally treated as the final consumers of materials incorporated into real property contracts and must pay 6% Provincial Sales Tax (PST) on all materials, tools, and equipment used in their work. When a contractor enters into a "supply-and-install" real property contract, they do not collect PST from the property owner on their labor or total contract price, provided PST was already paid on the underlying materials at the time of purchase.

The taxation of construction and trades in Saskatchewan is governed by *The Provincial Sales Tax Act* and detailed in the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance Information Bulletin PST-12 (*Information for Contractors and Subcontractors*). Understanding how PST applies to your specific contract structure is critical to avoid massive audit reassessments.

In Saskatchewan, contracts generally fall into one of two distinct categories:

### 1. Real Property "Supply-and-Install" Contracts
A real property contract involves constructing, altering, repairing, or improving land or buildings where materials become permanently affixed to the structure. Common examples include framing a new commercial building, installing electrical wiring, pouring concrete foundations, installing plumbing rough-ins, or roofing a residential home.

- **The Contractor's PST Obligation:** The contractor is the legal consumer of the materials. You must pay 6% PST to your building supply vendor when purchasing lumber, drywall, pipe, wire, shingles, and fixtures.
- **Billing the Client:** On your customer invoice, you do **not** charge 6% PST on the contract price, and you do **not** break out PST on the materials. Your invoice includes the agreed contract amount plus 5% federal GST. Your material costs (including the non-recoverable PST you paid) are factored directly into your job estimate and overall pricing.
- **Out-of-Province Purchases & Self-Assessment:** If you purchase construction materials from suppliers in Alberta, Manitoba, the United States, or online where Saskatchewan PST is not charged at the point of sale, you are legally required to self-assess and remit the 6% PST directly to the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance on your next provincial tax return.

### 2. Retail Sales and Over-the-Counter Repair Contracts
If your trade business sells uninstalled building materials, appliances, or off-the-shelf equipment directly to customers without installation, or if you perform pure repair services on tangible personal property (such as repairing a portable generator or servicing shop machinery that is not permanently affixed to real property), you are acting as a licensed vendor.

- **Purchasing for Resale:** You may purchase materials exempt from PST by providing your Saskatchewan PST Vendor Licence number to your supplier.
- **Billing the Client:** You must charge and collect 6% Saskatchewan PST and 5% GST on the total retail price charged to the customer, remitting the collected PST to the Ministry of Finance.

The comparison table below clarifies how Saskatchewan PST applies across common contractor operating scenarios:

| Transaction / Contract Type | PST on Material Purchase | PST Charged on Customer Invoice | GST Charged on Customer Invoice | Primary Governing Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supply-and-Install Residential Construction** | Contractor pays 6% PST to vendor | **NO PST** charged to client | 5% GST charged on total invoice | PST-12 Real Property Contract |
| **Supply-and-Install Commercial Tenant Buildout** | Contractor pays 6% PST to vendor | **NO PST** charged to client | 5% GST charged on total invoice | PST-12 Real Property Contract |
| **Retail Over-the-Counter Material Sale** | Exempt via PST Vendor Licence | **Charge 6% PST** on selling price | 5% GST charged on selling price | Standard Retail Sale of Goods |
| **Out-of-Province Material Purchase (No Tax Paid)** | Contractor must self-assess 6% PST | **NO PST** charged to client | 5% GST charged on total invoice | Mandatory Provincial Self-Assessment |
| **Subcontractor Invoicing General Contractor** | Subcontractor pays 6% PST on goods | **NO PST** charged to GC | 5% GST charged to GC | Trade Contract Rules Apply |

Failing to properly account for PST on building materials is the single most frequent reason Saskatchewan construction firms fail Ministry of Finance audits. Maintaining structured, dedicated accounts for PST expense and PST self-assessment in your general ledger prevents expensive retroactive tax bills.

## Why Are Saskatchewan WCB Clearance Letters Mandatory Before Paying Subcontractors?

Saskatchewan construction contractors must obtain a valid Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) clearance letter before releasing payment to any subcontractor because provincial legislation holds the hiring contractor legally liable for any unpaid WCB premiums owed by that subcontractor. If you pay a subtrade without an active clearance letter and they default on their WCB premiums, the WCB will legally assess your business for their unpaid compensation liabilities.

Under Section 131 of *The Workers' Compensation Act, 2013*, the Saskatchewan WCB has the statutory authority to recover unpaid assessment premiums from the principal contractor who hired the subcontractor. 

To safeguard your company from assuming someone else’s debt, your bookkeeping system must enforce a strict "No Clearance, No Payment" accounts payable policy:

1. **Requesting Clearances Electronically:** Before releasing any progress payment or final cheque to an electrical, plumbing, framing, or dry-walling subcontractor, log into your Saskatchewan WCB online account and generate an official Clearance Letter for the subcontractor’s legal business name and account number.
2. **Interpreting the Clearance Status:** 
   - **Letter of Clearance (Granted):** Confirms the subcontractor is registered, has active coverage, and their assessment account is fully paid and in good standing. You are legally cleared to release payment for the dates specified on the letter.
   - **Letter of Clearance (Denied):** Indicates the subcontractor is either not registered with the WCB, has overdue premiums, or has failed to submit their annual payroll declaration. You must immediately withhold payment from the subcontractor until clearance is resolved.
3. **Withholding Funds Upon Denial:** If the WCB issues a clearance denial, the hiring contractor is legally permitted—and required—to withhold funds from the subcontractor's invoice up to the estimated WCB assessment rate, remitting those funds directly to the WCB on behalf of the subcontractor to clear the account.

In addition to managing subcontractor clearances, trades contractors must manage their own annual WCB obligations:
- **Annual Payroll Reporting:** By February 28 each year, every registered employer must submit their Employer’s Annual Payroll Return, declaring actual gross assessable wages paid to workers in the previous calendar year and estimating payroll for the upcoming year.
- **Maximum Assessable Earnings:** The Saskatchewan WCB caps the maximum assessable earnings per individual worker (reviewed annually, reaching over \$99,000 in recent years). Earnings paid above this cap to an individual employee are exempt from WCB premiums.
- **Personal Coverage for Business Owners:** Incorporated directors and sole proprietors are not automatically covered by WCB unless they apply for Optional Personal Coverage. If an owner-operator works on active construction job sites, general contractors will universally demand proof of active personal WCB coverage before permitting them onto commercial sites.

## What Is a CRA T5018 Statement of Contract Payments and When Must It Be Filed?

A CRA T5018 Statement of Contract Payments is a mandatory annual tax information return that Canadian construction businesses must file to report payments made to subcontractors for construction services. Any business whose primary source of business income (more than 50%) is derived from construction activities must file a T5018 slip for every subcontractor to whom they paid \$500 or more in labor and mixed service payments during the reporting year.

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) established the Contract Payment Reporting System (CPRS) to combat the underground economy in the construction industry. The CRA cross-references the amounts you report on T5018 slips against the business income declared by your subcontractors on their T2 corporate tax returns or T1 personal tax returns.

### What Construction Activities Trigger T5018 Reporting?
Under CRA regulations, construction activities include erecting, excavating, installing, altering, repairing, demolishing, or structural remodeling of any building, road, bridge, or real property. This encompasses:
- General carpentry, framing, and drywalling
- Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC installations
- Roofing, masonry, siding, and exterior insulation
- Earthmoving, excavation, paving, and landscaping installation
- Painting, flooring, and interior finishing

### What Information Must Be Collected from Every Subcontractor?
To file accurate T5018 slips, your **bookkeeper in Saskatoon** must collect complete vendor documentation before any work begins. Best practice requires every subcontractor to submit a completed Subcontractor Information Profile containing:
- Legal Business Name and Operating Name (Trade Name)
- Full Mailing Address
- 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN) for incorporated subcontractors, or Social Insurance Number (SIN) for unincorporated sole proprietors
- Contact telephone number and authorized representative signature

### What Are the T5018 Filing Deadlines and CRA Penalties?
T5018 returns can be filed based on the calendar year or your company’s fiscal year. The complete return (consisting of individual T5018 slips and the T5018 Summary) must be filed with the CRA no later than **six months after the end of your chosen reporting period**. For example, if your construction company has a December 31 fiscal year-end, your T5018 filing deadline is June 30.

The CRA imposes harsh penalties for late filing or failing to submit T5018 returns. Under Section 162(7) of the *Income Tax Act*, penalties for failing to file information returns are calculated based on the number of slips and the number of days the return is overdue:

| Number of T5018 Slips Required | Daily CRA Penalty | Maximum Statutory Penalty |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 to 50 slips** | \$10 per day late | \$1,000 maximum penalty |
| **51 to 500 slips** | \$25 per day late | \$2,500 maximum penalty |
| **501 to 2,500 slips** | \$50 per day late | \$5,000 maximum penalty |
| **Over 2,500 slips** | \$100 per day late | \$7,500 maximum penalty |

Beyond late-filing fines, submitting incomplete slips with missing or invalid Business Numbers or SINs triggers additional CRA administrative penalties of \$100 per slip. Establishing a disciplined monthly subcontractor tracking system ensures every payment is logged and categorized correctly, eliminating year-end panic.

## Is a Trades Worker an Employee or an Independent Subcontractor Under CRA Rules?

Under Canada Revenue Agency rules, a trades worker's legal classification as an employee or independent subcontractor is determined by the factual reality of the working relationship, not by the label written in a contract or verbal agreement. The CRA evaluates four fundamental legal tests: the degree of employer control, ownership of tools and equipment, chance of profit or risk of loss, and integration into the commercial enterprise.

Many construction business owners mistakenly believe that having a worker sign a "Subcontractor Agreement" or invoice for their hours protects the business from employer obligations. If the CRA audits your company and determines that your "subcontractors" were legally employees, the financial repercussions are catastrophic.

The CRA will retroactively assess your business for:
- 100% of unwithheld employee Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions plus the mandatory employer match.
- 100% of unwithheld Employment Insurance (EI) premiums plus the 1.4x employer premium.
- Compounding daily arrears interest on all back taxes.
- Harsh penalties of 10% to 20% for failure to deduct and remit source deductions under Section 227(9) of the *Income Tax Act*.
- Retroactive reassessments from the Saskatchewan Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) for unpaid payroll premiums.

To ensure proper worker classification, evaluate your trades workforce against the four legal criteria established in Canadian common law:

| Legal Evaluation Factor | Independent Subcontractor Status | Direct Employee Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Degree of Control** | Decides how, when, and in what order work is completed; minimal supervision | Employer dictates daily schedule, work methods, and directly supervises tasks |
| **Ownership of Tools & Equipment** | Supplies major commercial tools, heavy equipment, work vehicle, and safety gear | Uses company-provided power tools, shop equipment, materials, and fleet vehicles |
| **Subcontracting & Delegation** | Free to hire helpers or subtrades to complete tasks without employer permission | Must perform the labor personally; cannot delegate work to an outside party |
| **Financial Risk & Profit Potential** | Bears risk of bad debts, project cost overruns, and warranty fixes; sets own rates | Paid a fixed hourly wage or salary; carries zero commercial risk or project liability |
| **Exclusivity & Integration** | Operates an independent business; advertises and serves multiple general contractors | Works exclusively for your company; represents your firm wearing branded uniforms |

If a worker works exclusively for your construction company, uses your tools, arrives at 7:30 AM every day under your foreman's supervision, and has no financial risk on the job, the CRA will classify that worker as an **employee**. Your bookkeeping and payroll system must treat them accordingly by establishing a CRA payroll account and remitting monthly source deductions.

## How Does Job Costing Help Saskatchewan Contractors Maintain Healthy Profit Margins?

Job costing is the disciplined bookkeeping method of allocating all direct expenditures—including site labor, building materials, equipment rentals, permits, and subtrade invoices—to specific construction projects in real time. For Saskatchewan contractors, job costing reveals the true gross profit margin of each individual project, allowing business owners to identify bidding inaccuracies, manage cost overruns, and avoid unprofitable contracts.

In the trades, high top-line revenue often creates a false sense of financial prosperity. A residential framing contractor or commercial HVAC company can easily generate \$1,500,000 in annual revenue while quietly losing money because individual jobs suffer from untracked labor overtime, wasted materials, and unbilled change orders.

Accurate job costing divides contractor expenditures into three distinct categories:

### 1. Direct Job Costs
These are expenses that occur solely because of a specific project:
- **Direct Materials:** Lumber, electrical conduit, plumbing fixtures, ready-mix concrete, insulation, and fasteners delivered to the specific job site.
- **Direct Field Labor:** Gross wages, employer payroll taxes, and WCB premiums paid to workers for the exact hours spent working on that project.
- **Subcontractor Fees:** Invoices paid to specialized subtrades (e.g., excavators, foundation specialists, tapers, roofers) allocated directly to the job.
- **Equipment Rentals:** Scissor lifts, excavators, site trailers, portable toilets, and scaffolding rented specifically for that site.

### 2. Indirect Job Costs
Costs that support multiple projects but cannot be attributed to a single phase:
- Small tool purchases and maintenance (blades, drill bits, hand tools under \$500)
- Waste disposal, commercial bin rentals, and site cleanup expenses
- Project superintendent salaries and field safety coordinators

### 3. General Operating Overhead
Fixed administrative expenses required to run the company, regardless of project volume:
- Commercial shop or office rent in Saskatoon (e.g., Marquis Industrial lease)
- Office administrative staff and professional bookkeeping fees
- Commercial liability insurance and commercial fleet vehicle leases
- Marketing, website hosting, and corporate legal expenses

### Calculating Job Profitability in Real Time
By tracking direct costs in cloud accounting software like QuickBooks Online or Xero paired with construction job-tracking apps (such as BuilderTrend, Jobber, or Knowify), contractors can compare actual expenses against initial bid estimates while the job is still active:

$$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Contract Revenue} - (\text{Direct Materials} + \text{Direct Labor} + \text{Subcontractors} + \text{Job Rentals})$$

$$\text{Gross Profit Margin (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Contract Revenue}} \right) \times 100$$

If your target gross margin is 35% but job costing reveals your framing crews are operating at 22% due to excessive site waste and overtime, you can adjust bids immediately on upcoming tenders rather than discovering the shortfall months later when reviewing year-end financial statements.

## How Do Progress Billing and Statutory Holdbacks Work Under The Saskatchewan Builders' Lien Act?

Progress billing is an invoicing method where contractors bill clients incrementally at key project milestones, while a statutory holdback is a mandatory 10% deduction withheld from each progress payment to protect against unpaid subtrades and material suppliers. In Saskatchewan, statutory holdbacks are legally governed by *The Builders' Lien Act*, requiring contractors to track holdbacks receivable and holdbacks payable on their balance sheet.

### Understanding the 10% Saskatchewan Statutory Holdback
Under Section 34 of *The Builders' Lien Act*, every person who is liable to pay for services or materials under a construction contract must retain a **holdback of 10%** of the gross value of the work done or materials furnished as work progresses.

- **Holdback Receivable (Asset):** When you issue a progress invoice of \$50,000 plus 5% GST to a general contractor or owner, they will pay you 90% of the invoice value (\$45,000 plus GST) and retain 10% (\$5,000) as a statutory holdback. That \$5,000 is not lost; it is recorded as a "Holdbacks Receivable" current asset on your Balance Sheet.
- **Holdback Payable (Liability):** Similarly, when your subtrades invoice your company for \$20,000 plus GST, you must retain 10% (\$2,000) and pay them \$18,000 plus GST. That \$2,000 is recorded as a "Holdbacks Payable" current liability on your Balance Sheet until statutory release requirements are met.

### Saskatchewan Prompt Payment Legislation
Saskatchewan enacted comprehensive prompt payment amendments under *The Builders' Lien (Prompt Payment) Amendment Act*. Under these rules:
- An owner must pay a contractor within **28 calendar days** of receiving a proper, detailed progress invoice.
- A general contractor must pay its subcontractors within **7 calendar days** of receiving payment from the owner.
- A subcontractor must pay its sub-subcontractors within **7 calendar days** of receiving payment.

If a party disputes an invoice, they must issue a formal Notice of Non-Payment within 14 days, detailing the exact grounds for the dispute while paying any undisputed portions immediately.

### Releasing the Statutory Holdback
The 10% holdback cannot be released simply because work is finished. Under Saskatchewan law, the holdback is held until:
1. A Certificate of Substantial Performance is officially posted and filed.
2. The statutory 40-day lien expiration period passes with no builders' liens registered against the title of the real property.
3. Updated WCB clearance letters are verified for all trades.

Your bookkeeping system must segregate regular Accounts Receivable from Holdbacks Receivable. Failing to separate holdbacks distorts your immediate working capital and aging reports, making it appear that clients are 60 or 90 days delinquent when, in reality, funds are legally frozen under provincial statute.

Maintaining impeccable financial records ensures that your construction company remains profitable, liquid, and audit-ready. Use this comprehensive monthly checklist to verify your operational and regulatory compliance:

### Monthly Trades Bookkeeping & Compliance Checklist
- [ ] **Collect and Digitize Material Invoices:** Capture every lumber, plumbing, electrical, and hardware supplier receipt using cloud scanning software, ensuring Saskatchewan PST is verified.
- [ ] **Audit Self-Assessed PST on Imports:** Identify any construction supplies or tools purchased from out-of-province vendors where PST was omitted, and calculate mandatory 6% self-assessment amounts.

- [ ] **Reconcile Commercial Bank and Credit Card Accounts:** Verify that every cleared bank transaction, supplier draft, and credit card swipe matches internal general ledgers with zero discrepancy.
- [ ] **Review Job Cost Margins:** Generate job profitability reports for all active projects, comparing actual labor hours and material consumption against initial tender estimates.
- [ ] **Remit Payroll Source Deductions and Sales Taxes:** Submit monthly CRA source deductions by the 15th, and file monthly Saskatchewan PST and GST returns prior to statutory deadlines.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Contractor Bookkeeping in Saskatchewan

### Do general contractors charge PST to homeowners on residential renovations in Saskatchewan?
No, on a supply-and-install residential renovation contract in Saskatchewan, the general contractor does not charge 6% PST to the homeowner on the contract invoice. Under Information Bulletin PST-12, the contractor is legally considered the final consumer of the building materials and must pay 6% PST to suppliers when purchasing materials. The contractor invoices the homeowner for the contract price plus 5% federal GST, embedding their material costs (and the PST paid on them) directly into their job quote.

### What is the deadline for filing T5018 subcontractor slips with the CRA?
The deadline for filing T5018 Statement of Contract Payments slips and the T5018 Summary with the Canada Revenue Agency is six months after the end of your reporting period. Construction companies can choose to report on a calendar year basis (due June 30) or based on their fiscal year-end (due exactly six months following fiscal year-end). Late filing triggers steep daily CRA penalties ranging from \$10 to \$100 per day, up to statutory maximums of \$7,500.

### Can a contractor deduct WCB premiums paid on behalf of a delinquent subcontractor?
Yes, under Section 131 of Saskatchewan's *The Workers' Compensation Act, 2013*, if a hiring contractor is forced to pay overdue WCB assessment premiums on behalf of a subcontractor because clearance was denied, the contractor has the legal right to deduct those paid amounts directly from any monies owed to that subcontractor, or to sue the subcontractor to recover the funds.

### What is the statutory holdback percentage in Saskatchewan construction contracts?
Under *The Builders' Lien Act* of Saskatchewan, the statutory holdback is 10% of the gross value of all services, labor, and materials provided under the contract. The hiring owner or general contractor must withhold this 10% from each progress payment until 40 days have elapsed following the issuance of a Certificate of Substantial Performance, provided no builders' liens have been registered against the property title.
