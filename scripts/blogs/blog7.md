## What Is Catch-Up and Cleanup Bookkeeping?

Catch-up and cleanup bookkeeping is the specialized administrative process of organizing, categorizing, and reconciling months or years of delinquent business financial transactions to reconstruct accurate, audit-ready financial ledgers. Unlike routine monthly maintenance, a bookkeeping cleanup untangles historical bank backlogs, digitizes missing receipts, tracks unrecorded expenses, separates commingled personal funds, and prepares the company for overdue CRA and provincial tax filings.

In Saskatoon and throughout Saskatchewan, a cleanup project transforms financial chaos into structured trial balances that can be submitted to your tax accountant or used directly for corporate and personal tax filings:

- **Historical Statement Acquisition:** Retrieving historical PDF and CSV bank, credit card, and loan statements from financial institutions like Affinity Credit Union, Conexus Credit Union, RBC, TD, or Scotiabank.
- **Source Document Reconstruction:** Tracking down supplier invoices, digitizing paper receipts, and matching merchant processing deposits from platforms like Square, Stripe, or Moneris.
- **General Ledger Categorization:** Building a compliant Chart of Accounts and assigning every historical transaction to its proper revenue or deductible expense category.
- **Bank and Account Reconciliation:** Verifying that every cleared transaction matches the general ledger to produce an exact zero-dollar variance.
- **Tax Liability Determination:** Accurately calculating historical federal Goods and Services Tax (GST), Saskatchewan Provincial Sales Tax (PST), and corporate or personal income tax obligations.

## What Are the Real Dangers of Overdue Books and Unfiled Taxes?

The real dangers of overdue books and unfiled taxes include arbitrary CRA tax assessments, gross negligence penalties, compounding interest, frozen commercial bank accounts, and the permanent loss of valuable tax deductions and Input Tax Credits. Neglecting your financial records also damages your credit standing, preventing you from obtaining commercial loans, equipment leases, or business expansion capital.

When bookkeeping falls multiple years behind, the consequences escalate from administrative inconvenience to severe existential threats to your enterprise:

### 1. CRA Arbitrary Tax Assessments (Notional Assessments)
If you fail to file your tax returns, the Canada Revenue Agency will not wait indefinitely. Under **Subsection 152(7) of the federal *Income Tax Act***, the CRA has the statutory authority to issue an **Arbitrary Assessment** (also called a "Notional Assessment").
- The CRA arbitrarily estimates your gross revenue based on industry averages, previous years' filings, or third-party bank deposit audits.
- **The CRA grants ZERO expense deductions** on an arbitrary assessment. If your trades business had $300,000 in gross revenue and $250,000 in legitimate materials and subcontractor expenses, the CRA will assess tax as if you kept the entire $300,000 as pure net profit.
- The resulting tax bill is often tens of thousands—or hundreds of thousands—of dollars higher than your true tax obligation. 
- Until you submit complete, reconciled books and filed returns to replace the arbitrary assessment, the CRA considers that inflated balance legally binding and enforceable.

### 2. Compounding Interest and Severe Penalties
The CRA and the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance apply compounding daily interest on overdue taxes, combined with aggressive late-filing penalties:
- **CRA Late-Filing Penalty:** 5% of your unpaid balance plus 1% per full month the return is late, up to a maximum of 12 months.
- **Repeated Failure to File Penalty:** If the CRA issued a formal demand to file in any of the three preceding tax years, the penalty doubles to **10% of the unpaid balance plus 2% per month**, up to a maximum of 20 months.
- **Subsection 163(2) Gross Negligence Penalties:** Can add a crushing **50% penalty** to the understated tax if the CRA determines that recordkeeping failures constituted willful blindness or gross negligence.

### 3. Permanent Forfeiture of Input Tax Credits (The 4-Year Rule)
Under the federal *Excise Tax Act*, there is a strict statutory **four-year limitation period** for claiming Input Tax Credits (ITCs) on business expenses. If your bookkeeping falls more than four years behind, any GST you paid on commercial tools, materials, vehicle fuel, and shop rent during those older periods is **permanently lost**. You can never claim those refunds back from the CRA.

### 4. Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance PST Enforcement
Unlike the CRA, which handles federal matters, the Saskatchewan Revenue Division moves rapidly when PST returns are delinquent:
- Immediate 10% late payment penalty on all unremitted provincial sales tax.
- Issuance of formal third-party Requirement to Pay notices, freezing corporate bank accounts at local financial institutions.
- Suspension or revocation of your Saskatchewan PST Vendor Licence, making it illegal for your company to conduct commercial business in the province.

### 5. Inability to Secure Bank Financing or Commercial Leases
Whether approaching Affinity Credit Union, Conexus Credit Union, or a chartered bank for a commercial line of credit, lenders require up-to-date Balance Sheets, Income Statements, and Notices of Assessment. Without clean bookkeeping, securing equipment financing, commercial mortgages, or Business Development Bank of Canada (BDC) loans is completely impossible.

## What Records Must a Saskatchewan Business Keep for CRA Compliance?

A Saskatchewan business must keep all original source documents, including client sales invoices, vendor receipts, bank and credit card statements, payroll records, and tax filings, for a minimum of six full years from the end of the relevant tax year. If a return is filed late, the mandatory six-year retention period begins on the actual date the late return was submitted to the Canada Revenue Agency.

Under CRA Information Circular **IC78-10R5 (Books and Records Retention/Destruction)**, maintaining compliant source records is a strict legal requirement. The table below outlines the core documents required for a complete cleanup:

| Record Category | Required Documentation for Cleanup | Purpose in Cleanup Project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Banking Records** | Complete 12-month PDF statements & digital CSV exports | Foundation for reconciling every dollar entering or leaving the business |
| **Credit Card Records** | Business and personal credit card statements used for expenses | Verifying deductible operational purchases and recurring subscriptions |
| **Sales Invoices & POS** | Invoices issued, cash register tapes, Stripe/Square logs | Substantiating declared gross revenue and sales taxes collected |
| **Supplier Invoices** | Receipts, supplier billing statements, proof of payment | Substantiating operating deductions and supporting GST ITC claims |
| **Capital Asset Purchases** | Vehicle bills of sale, machinery invoices, lease contracts | Establishing Capital Cost Allowance (CCA) depreciation schedules |
| **Payroll & Subcontractors** | T4 summaries, source deduction remittances, T5018 slips | Reconciling employee wages and CRA construction subcontractor reporting |
| **Corporate Legal Records** | Articles of Incorporation, ISC annual returns, minute book | Confirming legal structure, registered office, and director details |

## Can You Keep Business Receipts Digitally in Saskatchewan?

Yes, under Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) guidelines, Saskatchewan businesses can legally keep business receipts digitally instead of retaining original paper copies. Digital images, mobile scans, and electronic PDF receipts are fully valid for CRA tax deduction audits, provided the digital copies remain clear, legible, authentic, unalterable, and securely stored for the mandatory six-year retention period.

## How to Deal with the CRA When Your Books Are Years Behind

When your books are years behind, the worst mistake is remaining silent while the CRA issues notices and demands; instead, you should contact the CRA or have an authorized representative establish communication to request a filing extension while your books are being reconstructed. Taking proactive steps demonstrates good faith and frequently prevents the CRA from freezing bank accounts or initiating formal legal actions.

Two vital CRA administrative relief mechanisms can protect Saskatchewan taxpayers who come forward voluntarily:

### 1. The CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP)
If you have unfiled tax returns or unreported business income and the CRA has not yet initiated formal audit or compliance enforcement action against you, you may qualify for the **CRA Voluntary Disclosures Program (VDP)**:
- **Penalty Relief:** If accepted under the General Program, the CRA waives 100% of late-filing and gross negligence penalties that would otherwise apply.
- **Interest Relief:** The CRA may grant up to 50% partial relief on accrued compounding interest for older taxation years.
- **Criminal Immunity:** A valid disclosure grants immunity from criminal tax prosecution.
- *Crucial Requirement:* To qualify, the disclosure must be voluntary, complete, involve an application of penalty, and include information that is at least one year past due.

### 2. CRA Taxpayer Relief Provisions
Under Subsection 220(3.1) of the *Income Tax Act*, the CRA has discretionary authority to cancel or waive penalties and interest when extraordinary circumstances beyond your control prevented you from filing on time:
- Severe personal illness, accident, or hospitalization.
- Serious mental distress, bereavement, or death in the immediate family.
- Natural or man-made disasters (such as a fire, flood, or severe postal disruption).
- CRA errors, service delays, or inaccurate administrative guidance.
