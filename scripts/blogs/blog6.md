## What Is the Difference Between GST and PST in Saskatchewan?

The difference between GST and PST in Saskatchewan is that the Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a 5% federal value-added tax administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), while the Provincial Sales Tax (PST) is a 6% retail sales tax administered directly by the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance via the Saskatchewan e-Tax Service (SETS). Together, they establish a combined sales tax rate of 11% on taxable goods and services across the province.

While both taxes appear on customer invoices, they function under fundamentally different legislative frameworks, tax accounting rules, and audit methodologies:

- **Federal Goods and Services Tax (GST - 5%):** GST is a classic value-added tax governed by the *Excise Tax Act*. It applies to most goods and services across Canada. Registered businesses collect 5% GST from clients, claim **Input Tax Credits (ITCs)** to recover all GST paid on business operating expenditures, and remit only the net difference to the CRA.
- **Saskatchewan Provincial Sales Tax (PST - 6%):** PST is a single-incident consumer retail tax governed by *The Provincial Sales Tax Act* of Saskatchewan. Registered vendors collect 6% PST on retail sales of taxable goods and specified services. Unlike GST, **there are no Input Tax Credits for PST**. Instead, businesses pay non-refundable PST on goods or services consumed in their own operations, while purchasing goods strictly intended for commercial resale tax-free using a valid PST vendor licence.

## When Is a Saskatchewan Business Required to Register for a GST Number?

A Saskatchewan business is legally required to register for a federal GST number with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) when its worldwide gross taxable sales exceed $30,000 over any single calendar quarter or within four consecutive calendar quarters. Businesses earning $30,000 or less are classified as "small suppliers" and are exempt from mandatory GST registration, although voluntary registration is permitted.

Under Section 240 of the federal *Excise Tax Act*, tracking your gross revenue is a continuous legal obligation. You must register within 29 days of the transaction that pushes your revenue over the $30,000 threshold.

### Should You Voluntarily Register for GST as a Small Supplier?
Even if your business generates less than $30,000 annually, voluntary GST registration offers powerful financial advantages:
1. **Recovering GST on Startup Expenses (ITCs):** If you are launching a business and purchasing tools, machinery, commercial vehicles, office computers, or inventory, you pay 5% GST on every purchase. If you are registered for GST, the CRA refunds 100% of the GST you paid through Input Tax Credits. If you are not registered, that 5% is a permanently lost expenditure.
2. **Commercial B2B Credibility:** Large corporate clients and institutional entities expect vendors to have a valid CRA Business Number with an active GST program account (`RT0001`). Lacking a GST number signals to commercial clients that your business generates less than $30,000 annually, which can undermine negotiations for high-value contracts.
3. **No Financial Cost to Commercial Clients:** Because your commercial B2B clients also claim ITCs, the 5% GST you charge them is fully refunded to them by the CRA—meaning charging GST does not make your services more expensive to commercial businesses.

## What Is the Saskatchewan PST "Zero-Threshold" Rule?

The Saskatchewan PST zero-threshold rule dictates that any business carrying on commercial activities in Saskatchewan that sells, leases, or provides taxable goods or services must register as a licensed PST vendor with the Ministry of Finance from dollar one, regardless of annual sales volume. Unlike the federal GST framework, Saskatchewan legislation provides no small supplier revenue exemption for provincial sales tax.

This is the single most common compliance trap for Saskatchewan entrepreneurs. Many founders mistakenly assume that because they earn under $30,000 and do not need a GST number, they are also exempt from PST. This assumption is completely false.

### Statutory Scope of Saskatchewan PST
Under *The Provincial Sales Tax Act*, you must obtain a Saskatchewan PST Vendor Licence before making your first retail sale of:
- **Tangible Personal Property:** Physical goods, manufactured products, retail merchandise, vehicles, mobile equipment, and consumer goods sold in Saskatchewan.
- **Computer Software & Digital Services:** Software-as-a-Service (SaaS), prewritten computer programs, digital media downloads, cloud subscriptions, and electronic applications accessed by customers located in Saskatchewan.
- **Commercial Construction & Real Property Services:** Since provincial legislative reforms, PST applies to labour and materials on commercial construction, repair, and renovation contracts.
- **Professional & Commercial Services:** Security services, credit reporting, dry cleaning, telecommunications, commercial legal services, architectural and engineering services, and accounting or bookkeeping advisory.

If you make even a single $100 taxable retail sale to a customer located in Saskatchewan, you are legally required to hold a PST vendor licence, collect $6 in PST, and remit it to the Ministry of Finance.

## How Do Input Tax Credits (ITCs) Work for Federal GST?

Input Tax Credits (ITCs) are the statutory mechanism through which registered businesses recover the 5% GST paid on eligible business operating expenses, capital assets, and commercial inputs. By subtracting total ITCs paid from total GST collected from customers, a business remits only the net difference to the CRA—or receives a cash refund if ITCs exceed collections.

The basic tax accounting formula is straightforward:

$$\text{Net GST Remittance} = \text{Total GST Collected on Sales} - \text{Total GST Paid on Business Purchases (ITCs)}$$

### Real-World ITCs Calculation Example
Imagine your Saskatoon electrical contracting business records the following financial activity during a quarterly reporting period:
- **Commercial Invoiced Sales:** $80,000 + 5% GST ($4,000) = $84,000 collected.
- **Commercial Van Purchase:** $40,000 + 5% GST ($2,000 paid).
- **Electrical Supplies & Inventory:** $15,000 + 5% GST ($750 paid).
- **Shop Rent, Fuel & Software:** $5,000 + 5% GST ($250 paid).
- **Total GST Collected:** $4,000.
- **Total ITCs Claimed:** $2,000 + $750 + $250 = **$3,000**.
- **Net GST Remitted to CRA:** $4,000 - $3,000 = **$1,000**.

If your business purchases major capital equipment during a quarter and your ITCs exceed your GST collected, the CRA issues a direct deposit refund check for the difference.

### What Documentation Does the CRA Require to Support ITCs?
You cannot claim an ITC based solely on a credit card statement or bank withdrawal. Under CRA Information Circulars, every claimed ITC must be substantiated by a detailed vendor receipt or invoice displaying:

```
[ ] Supplier's Legal Name or Operating Business Name
[ ] Date the invoice was issued or transaction occurred
[ ] Total Amount Paid or Payable for the supply
[ ] Supplier's 9-digit CRA Business Number (BN / GST Registration Number)
[ ] Clear statement of the GST charged (or explicit note that total price includes 5% GST)
[ ] For purchases over $500: Buyer's legal name, terms of payment, and detailed description
```

If an invoice lacks the supplier's valid GST registration number, the CRA will summarily disallow the ITC during a review, forcing you to repay the tax with compounding interest.

## Why Can Businesses Not Claim Input Tax Credits for Saskatchewan PST?

Businesses cannot claim Input Tax Credits for Saskatchewan PST because PST is structured as a single-incident consumer retail tax rather than a value-added tax. Under Saskatchewan law, the ultimate end-user or consumer must pay the 6% PST, and businesses that purchase goods or services for consumption in their own day-to-day operations are legally classified as final consumers.

This fundamental distinction dictates how purchases are treated:

```
│         FEDERAL GST (5%)          │     SASKATCHEWAN PST (6%)          │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Paid on all business purchases  │ • Resale Inventory: EXEMPT from PST│
│ • Fully recovered via ITCs        │   (Quote PST Vendor Licence)       │
│ • Recorded on Balance Sheet as an │ • Operating Consumables: PAY PST   │
│   asset (GST Receivable)          │   (PST paid is non-refundable)     │

│ • Zero net cost to business       │   (PST added to capital cost)      │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Resale Exemption Certificate
If your business purchases merchandise, raw materials, or components that will be directly resold to customers or incorporated into products manufactured for retail sale, you do **not** pay PST to your wholesale supplier. 
- You provide your wholesale vendor with your valid **Saskatchewan PST Vendor Licence Number**.
- The wholesaler invoices you without charging the 6% PST.
- When you ultimately sell the finished product to the retail consumer, you collect the 6% PST and remit it to the Ministry of Finance.
- However, if you purchase an office desk, computer monitor, or work vehicle for your staff, those items are consumed by your business; you must pay the 6% PST, and that tax cannot be refunded.

## What Are the Mandatory Sales Tax Filing Frequencies and Deadlines?

Sales tax filing frequencies and deadlines are assigned independently by the Canada Revenue Agency for GST and by the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance for PST, based on your annual taxable sales volume. Confusing these two reporting schedules is a frequent cause of late-filing penalties.

### 1. Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) GST Filing Deadlines
The CRA assigns your GST reporting frequency based on your annual taxable supplies across Canada:

| Annual Taxable Sales Threshold | Assigned GST Reporting Frequency | GST Return and Payment Due Date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **$1,500,000 or Less** | **Annual** (Can elect Quarterly or Monthly) | Exactly **3 months** after corporate fiscal year-end (or June 15 for individuals; payment due April 30) |
| **$1,500,001 to $6,000,000** | **Quarterly** (Can elect Monthly) | Exactly **1 month** following the close of the calendar quarter |
| **Over $6,000,000** | **Monthly** | Exactly **1 month** following the close of the calendar month |

*Example:* For a quarterly GST filer whose second quarter ends June 30, the GST return and full remittance payment must be received by the CRA no later than **July 31**.

### 2. Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance PST Filing Deadlines
The Saskatchewan Revenue Division assigns your PST filing cadence based on the anticipated or historical amount of provincial sales tax collected annually:

| Annual PST Collected | Assigned PST Reporting Frequency | PST Return & Payment Due Date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Less than $1,200 / year** | **Annual** | **January 20** of the following calendar year |
| **$1,200 to $6,000 / year** | **Quarterly** | **20th day** of the month following the calendar quarter |
| **Over $6,000 / year** | **Monthly** | **20th day** of the month following the calendar month |

> [!WARNING]
> Notice the critical difference: **GST payments are generally due on the final day of the following month, while Saskatchewan PST payments are due on the 20th of the following month.** Missing the 20th deadline on SETS triggers an immediate 10% penalty plus compounding provincial interest.

## What Happens If a Business Fails to Remit Collected GST and PST? (The Deemed Trust Rule)

If a business fails to remit collected GST and PST, the Canada Revenue Agency and the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance apply statutory "deemed trust" rules, deeming all collected taxes to be held in trust for His Majesty the King. In the eyes of the law, collected sales taxes never belonged to the business; failing to remit them is treated as an illegal diversion of government funds, rendering corporate directors personally liable.

## What Records Must a Saskatchewan Business Keep for Sales Tax Audits?

The Canada Revenue Agency and the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance require every business to retain complete source records, customer sales invoices, supplier receipts, and bank statements for a minimum of six full taxation years. In the event of a sales tax audit, the burden of proof rests entirely on the business owner to prove that sales were recorded accurately and that claimed ITCs or PST exemptions were legitimate.

Under CRA Information Circular **IC78-10R5 (Books and Records Retention/Destruction)**, electronic records and scanned digital receipts are fully accepted as legal source documentation, provided they are:
1. **Clear and Legible:** Scans and photographs must capture all text, dates, invoice numbers, and vendor tax identifiers with zero distortion.
2. **Unaltered and Authentic:** Digital files must not be digitally manipulated or altered.
3. **Easily Accessible:** Records must be readily retrievable and reproducible upon request by CRA or Ministry examiners.
4. **Securely Backed Up:** Cloud-hosted archives must be protected against data loss or corruption.

### Sales Tax Audit Readiness Checklist
Keep your business prepared for provincial and federal tax auditors by maintaining this digital documentation structure:

## Frequently Asked Questions About Saskatchewan GST and PST

### When does a Saskatchewan business need to register for a GST number?
A Saskatchewan business must register for a federal GST account with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) when its gross worldwide taxable sales exceed $30,000 across four consecutive calendar quarters or within a single calendar quarter. If gross sales remain at or below $30,000, registration is optional under the CRA "small supplier" exemption. However, registering voluntarily allows businesses to claim Input Tax Credits (ITCs) and recover 100% of the 5% GST paid on commercial equipment, supplies, and startup expenses.

### Does Saskatchewan have a small supplier exemption for PST?
No. Saskatchewan does not have a small supplier revenue exemption for Provincial Sales Tax (PST). Under *The Provincial Sales Tax Act*, any business selling taxable goods or providing taxable services within Saskatchewan must register as a licensed vendor with the Saskatchewan Ministry of Finance and collect 6% PST from dollar one. Even if your business earns only $500 in sales, you are legally required to hold a PST vendor licence and remit provincial tax on taxable transactions.

### Can a business get a refund for PST paid on operating expenses?
No. Unlike federal GST, which allows businesses to recover tax paid on business inputs through Input Tax Credits (ITCs), Saskatchewan PST is a single-incident retail sales tax on the final consumer. Businesses must pay non-refundable 6% PST on tools, office supplies, equipment, and services consumed within their own commercial operations. The only purchases exempt from PST are goods purchased strictly for commercial resale or raw materials incorporated into manufactured goods for resale, provided the business quotes its valid PST vendor licence to the supplier.

### What are the filing deadlines for GST and PST in Saskatchewan?
For quarterly filers, federal GST returns and payments are due to the CRA no later than the final day of the month following the end of the reporting quarter (e.g., April 30 for Q1 ending March 31). Annual GST filers with a corporate fiscal year-end must file and pay within three months of year-end. In contrast, Saskatchewan PST returns and payments submitted via SETS are due on the **20th day** of the month following the reporting period (e.g., April 20 for Q1 ending March 31).
