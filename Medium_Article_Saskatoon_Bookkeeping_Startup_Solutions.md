## The Triple Compliance Stack for Saskatchewan Corporations

Running an incorporated business in Saskatchewan requires staying on top of three separate, ongoing administrative tracks:

### 1. Monthly Financial Reconciliation & Bookkeeping
Accurate bookkeeping is not merely about surviving tax season; it is about financial clarity. In accordance with CRA standards, every business operating in Canada must maintain organized books and records for at least six years. Having [professional bookkeeping services in Saskatoon](https://saskstartupsolutions.ca/services) guarantees that monthly bank and credit card reconciliations, vendor expense categorization, and financial statements are consistently kept current.

### 2. T2 Corporate Income Tax Filing
A common trap for new founders is assuming that an inactive corporation or a business with no net income does not need to file a tax return. In reality, the CRA mandates that every Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) must file an annual T2 Corporate Return within six months of its fiscal year-end—and any tax balances payable are due even earlier (typically within 2 to 3 months). Missing these deadlines triggers steep late-filing penalties and non-deductible interest.

### 3. Saskatchewan Information Services Corporation (ISC) Registry Administration
Unlike federal-only businesses, Saskatchewan corporations must maintain active standing with the provincial ISC Corporate Registry. Failing to file your mandatory ISC Annual Return or neglecting to report changes to your corporate address or director roster can result in administrative strike-off and corporate dissolution.
